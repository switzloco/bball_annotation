## 5. Lessons Learned: How to Do Basketball Play-by-Play Reliably Without Too Much Argon

Based on both our prior architecture in `C:\Users\Nick\Documents\GitHub\bball_annotation` ([TILED_VISION_ARCHITECTURE.md](file:///C:/Users/Nick/Documents/GitHub/bball_annotation/TILED_VISION_ARCHITECTURE.md), [video_engine.py](file:///C:/Users/Nick/Documents/GitHub/bball_annotation/video_engine.py), [agent.py](file:///C:/Users/Nick/Documents/GitHub/bball_annotation/agent.py)) and our empirical execution on this 37.5-minute 4K dataset, here is why raw full-frame video fails and how our **Two-Stage Hybrid Pipeline** solves it with minimal token overhead:

### 1. Why Naive Full-Frame Video Fails (The 258-Token Resolution Bottleneck)
- When a standard 16:9 4K (`3840x2160`) video is passed directly to a multimodal LLM, each video frame is downsampled into a ~258-token thumbnail (~`768x432` effective resolution).
- In a single-camera full-court basketball setup:
  - The **Near Hoop** (`x=1474, y=282` in 4K) occupies less than `3%` of the frame area.
  - The **Far Hoop** (`x=1858, y=545` in 4K) occupies less than **`0.5%` of the frame area** (the rim is only ~18 pixels wide in native 4K, which shrinks to **< 3 pixels** after thumbnail downsampling!).
- Furthermore, standard `1 fps` temporal sampling frequently misses the `0.15s–0.25s` window where the ball passes through the net versus bouncing off the front rim.

### 2. Stage 1: Dual-Hoop 4K Picture-in-Picture (PiP) Proxy Segments (Coarse Discovery)
Instead of feeding raw 8.4 GB 4K video or losing the far court to downsampling, we use `ffmpeg` hardware/multi-threaded filtering to generate **3-minute `1280x720` @ `10 fps` PiP proxy segments** (~`22 MB` each) with burned-in `MM:SS.ms` global timestamps and **two permanent native-4K 1:1 crop overlays** pinned to the top corners:
- **Top-Left Overlay (`420x300`)**: Native 4K crop of the **Near Hoop** (`crop=640:460:1150:120`).
- **Top-Right Overlay (`420x300`)**: Native 4K crop of the **Far Hoop** (`crop=380:270:1670:440`, upscaled to `420x300`).
- **Bottom Main View**: Full-court panorama + AAC audio track (capturing player score callouts, foul calls, and net/rim acoustics).
- **Token Savings**: Watching thirteen 3-minute PiP segments gives 100% possession continuity, player tracking, and exact timestamps (`±0.5s`) for every shot attempt, turnover, and rebound at a fraction of raw video cost.

### 3. Stage 2: 4fps Native-4K Rim-Zoom Contact Sheets (100% Make/Miss Verification)
Whenever Stage 1 flags a shot attempt at timestamp $t_0$:
- We run a fast Python/FFmpeg extractor (`shot_zoom.py`) that crops the native 4K `.MOV`/`.mp4` directly around the active hoop (`Near` or `Far`) at **`4 fps` over a `7.0s` window (`28 frames` in a `4x7` high-res JPEG grid)**.
- Because a single static image uses high-resolution vision tiling rather than `258`-token video frame compression, **one single image read** lets the model inspect 28 sequential `0.25s` frames of the ball approaching the rim, hitting the backboard/iron, deforming the net cords, and being rebounded.
- **Result**: Every single make/miss, putback, and rebound across all 3 games (including distant Far-Hoop possessions in Game 3) was verified with zero ambiguity and minimal token expenditure.
